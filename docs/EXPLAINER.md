# GooCampus Portal — How It All Works (Plain-English Guide)

_A cover-to-cover explanation of the whole system in everyday language. No code, no
jargon. If you're new to GooCampus or just want to understand what the portal does and
why, start here. For the technical version, see the other files in this folder._

---

## 1. What this is, in one breath

GooCampus helps Indian doctors go abroad — the UK (PLAB), Australia (AMC), the Gulf (UAE),
and other pathways. Getting a doctor from "interested" to "practising overseas" involves a
sales conversation, a signed package, a lot of paperwork, exams, coaching, payments in
instalments, and months of hand-holding by the operations team.

**The portal is the single place where all of that happens.** Sales use it to close deals.
Clients use it to register and track their journey. Operations use it to deliver the
service step by step. HR, Finance, and management use it to run the company. Instead of
scattered spreadsheets and WhatsApp chats, everything lives in one system with one record
per client.

Think of it as the company's **operating system** — one login, many rooms.

---

## 2. The one idea that ties everything together

Everything in the portal revolves around **the journey of a single client**, from the first
sales call to the day they're settled abroad. If you understand that journey, you
understand the whole system. Here it is, start to finish:

> **A sales rep talks to a doctor → closes the deal → the doctor gets an invitation to
> register → the doctor fills in their details and uploads documents → sales and operations
> double-check everything → the client becomes an official "live" client → operations
> delivers their pathway over the coming months → at each stage we ask the client for
> honest feedback.**

Every section of the portal is just a tool that supports one part of that journey. Let's
walk through it.

---

## 3. The client journey, step by step

### Step 1 — The sales lead
A potential client (a doctor) starts as a **lead** in the Sales section. The rep logs
calls, moves the lead through stages ("interested", "negotiating", "won"), and works to
close it.

### Step 2 — Closing the deal → the invitation
When the rep closes the deal, they fill in a **closure form**: which product/pathway the
doctor bought, the **plan type**, the **package amount** (the price), any **discount**, and
how they'll pay (in instalments). The moment they save this, the portal automatically
**sends the doctor an invitation** — by email and WhatsApp — with a personal link to
register. This is the hand-off from sales to the client.

### Step 3 — The client registers
The doctor clicks their link, sets a password, and lands in the **client portal**. They
fill in a registration form in a few steps:
- **Personal details** — name, phone (with a country-code picker), address, family contacts.
- **Academic details** — this part is smart: it only shows the questions that apply. Ask
  "IMG or FMG?" and the right follow-up questions appear; "MBBS completed?" reveals
  internship questions; and so on. The doctor never sees fields that don't apply to them.
- **Documents** — they upload what's needed (photo, certificates).

### Step 4 — Two sets of eyes verify it
Before a client goes "live", two teams check the file:
- **Sales verification** — the sales team confirms the money details (final package,
  balance, plan) are correct.
- **Operations verification** — the ops team does the final check and clicks "verify".

That final click is important: it **creates the official master client record**. Up to this
point the client was a "registration in progress"; now they're a real, live client the
operations team will look after.

### Step 5 — Operations delivers the pathway
Now the long part begins. Depending on the pathway, operations tracks and delivers dozens
of things over months: **call notes** (every conversation), **payments** (instalments as
they come in), **exam bookings** (PLAB 1/2, AMC), **coaching**, **document/registration
milestones** (EPIC, GMC, AMC registration), visa, job stage, and more. Each of these is a
tidy little section on the client's profile page, so anyone can open a client and see
exactly where they are.

### Step 6 — Feedback at every stage
As the client passes through stages, the portal sends them a short, **anonymous** feedback
form (by email and WhatsApp). Anonymous so they answer honestly. The results tell
management how well the operations team is performing at each stage — with scores like CSAT
and NPS — and even show, in near-real-time, who received the message, who read it, and who
filled it in.

**That's the whole spine.** Everything below is either a tool that supports this journey, or
a support function (HR, Finance) that keeps the company running.

## 4. The sections, explained plainly

The portal is organised into sections, shown down the left as you move around. Here's what
each one is for.

### Sales
Where reps live day to day. They manage **leads**, record **closures**, track **targets**,
and — the key action — run the **closure form** that turns a won lead into a registered
client. There's also a **Revenue Report** that shows how much actual sales revenue each team
member has generated (their packages, minus cost, minus discounts they gave).

### Operations
The biggest and busiest section. It handles the **six pathways** — PLAB, Australia,
Consulting, UAE, Portfolio, and Training — each with its own list of clients and a detailed
profile page per client. On that profile, operations records everything: calls, payments,
exams, coaching, registrations, and so on. There's also a **verification** step here (the
final "go live" check) and reports like the **Call Notes report** (how many follow-up calls
each team member made, broken down by stage).

### Client Portal
The client's own view. After registering, a client logs in to see their **plan and
counsellor**, their **payment schedule** (what's paid, what's due, with GST shown), upload
their **photograph**, and read/sign the **refund policy**. It's their window into their
journey.

### Feedback
The anonymous, stage-wise feedback system described above. Management picks a stage, sends
the feedback link to the relevant clients (individually or in bulk over WhatsApp), and
reads the results — with a delivery report showing exactly what reached each client.

### Products & Packages
The "menu" behind the sales. Each **product** (e.g. AMC Consulting) has **plan types**, and
each plan has a **package price** and a **cost**, plus the list of **services included**.
This is where pricing and what-you-get is defined; sales and the client portal read from it.

### HR
Everything people-related for staff (not clients): **leave** (apply, approve, and the
monthly salary-deduction calculation), **attendance** (imported from the biometric
machine), **work-from-home** and **official travel** requests, **holidays**, and the
**employee directory**. The company's leave year runs April to March.

### KRA
Performance management. Managers set **goals** (KRAs) per role, assign them for the year,
and each month the employee rates themselves and the manager rates them. It feeds appraisals.

### Finance
The money-planning brain. **Budgets** (planned vs actual, month by month), **revenue**
tracking, **salaries** and **subscriptions**, and **per-project profit & loss**. It handles
multiple currencies and converts everything to rupees using live exchange rates.

### Partners
For referral partners (agencies, schools) who send GooCampus students. They get their own
mini-portal to submit and track leads, and the company sees all partner activity in one place.

### Colleges
A knowledge and lead-gen area: a **college directory**, an **MBBS cut-off predictor**, and a
public **NEET-PG document portal** where prospective students can request exam PDFs (and
become leads).

### WhatsApp
Bulk messaging: campaigns, contact lists, and message templates — sent through the same
WhatsApp provider (Infobip) used for invitations and feedback.

### Company & Clients admin
Housekeeping and control: **holidays**, **locations** (the master list of states and
cities), **announcements**, **email templates**, the **payments hub**, **refunds**,
**internal transfers** (moving a client from one pathway to another), and — importantly —
**Access Master** (below).

## 5. How money works (in plain terms)

- Every plan has a **package price** (what the client pays) and a **cost** (what it costs
  GooCampus to deliver).
- Clients often get a **discount**, so their **final package = price − discount**. That's the
  number the client actually owes.
- **GST is always 18%.** Internally the package is stored without GST; when clients pay in
  **instalments**, GST is added on top and shown clearly (Amount + 18% GST = Total).
- For management, **Sales Revenue = price − cost**, and **Actual Sales Revenue = Sales Revenue
  − discount**. That last figure — what a rep truly brought in after cost and discount — is
  what the Sales Revenue Report shows per team member. (Costs are only entered per plan; the
  client's "final package" number itself never includes cost.)

## 6. Who can see what (Access Master)

Not everyone should see everything. The portal has a permission system called **Access
Master**: for each person and each section, you grant "can view / can edit / can add". A
salesperson sees Sales; an ops delivery specialist sees their pathway; and so on.

One important quirk to remember: **administrators bypass all permission checks** — they see
everything. So if you (as an admin) test a screen it always works, even if a normal staff
member would be blocked. The rule of thumb: **when changing permissions, test as a normal
user, not as an admin.**

There are three kinds of login: **staff** (employees), **clients** (the doctors), and
**partners** (referral agencies) — each with their own front door.

## 7. How it all runs (the plumbing, kept simple)

- The whole thing is one web application written in **Python (Flask)** with a **PostgreSQL**
  database. It's server-rendered — plain web pages, no fancy app framework — which keeps it
  simple and reliable.
- It's hosted on **Render** (a cloud host). There are two copies: a **live** one that clients
  and staff use, and a **staging** one for testing changes before they go live. Both share
  one database.
- The live site is **goocampus.org**. (Note: goocampus.in is a *different*, separate
  marketing website — the portal is always goocampus.org.)
- It sends **email** through Resend and **WhatsApp** through Infobip, and stores documents on
  Cloudflare's file storage.
- New database fields and tables are created automatically when the app starts up, so
  deploying an update is just a push-and-go.

**If something ever breaks or the developer tools are unavailable:** the technical recovery
steps are in `DEPLOY.md` in this same folder — how to deploy, the settings needed, and how
to reach the live database safely.

## 8. A few important habits baked into the system

- **Times** are stored in a universal timezone but shown to the team in **IST** (Indian time).
- **The client's record is created only at ops-verification** — before that they're a
  "registration in progress". This is why, for example, a brand-new client can still upload a
  photo even before they're fully verified (the system quietly handles both cases).
- **The financial year runs April to March** everywhere (leave, budgets, KRA).
- **One client, one record.** After verification, a client has a single master record (used
  across every pathway), which is why nothing gets lost as they progress.

## 9. Mini-glossary

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lead** | A potential client the sales team is working on. |
| **Closure** | A won deal — the point a lead becomes a paying client. |
| **Registration** | The client's own filling-in of their details (in progress until verified). |
| **Pathway** | The route a doctor is taking (PLAB / Australia / UAE / Consulting / Portfolio / Training). |
| **Package** | The price of the client's plan. |
| **Final package** | Package minus discount — what the client owes. |
| **Sales revenue** | Package minus cost (the value to the company). |
| **Actual sales revenue** | Sales revenue minus discount — the rep's true contribution. |
| **Verification** | The two-step check (sales then ops) before a client goes live. |
| **Stage** | Where a client is in their pathway (e.g. exam booked, job stage). |
| **CSAT / NPS** | Two standard satisfaction scores from the feedback forms. |
| **Access Master** | The permission system that controls who sees which section. |
| **Instalment** | A part-payment of the package (GST added on top). |
