MBBS in Georgia is a 6-year English-medium MD (Doctor of Medicine) programme offered by NMC-approved universities — including BAU International University (Batumi), Grigol Robakidze University, East European University, Georgian American University (GAU) and Georgian National University (SEU). Annual tuition ranges from $4,800 to $6,500; the all-in 6-year total including hostel, food and insurance typically falls between ₹40 lakh and ₹54 lakh. Admission does not require IELTS, but a valid NEET qualifying score is mandatory for Indian students. Georgia is currently considered one of the strongest MBBS-abroad destinations on country-level FMGE performance trends, with two intakes per year — September and March. All universities listed are verified on WDOMS.

Frequently Asked

Questions, Answered.

The most common questions students and parents ask about MBBS in Georgia — direct answers, no hedging.

Is NEET compulsory for MBBS in Georgia for Indian students?
Yes — treat it as non-negotiable. NMC ties a valid NEET qualifying score to the India-return licensing route. Without it, you cannot sit the NExT examination in India after graduating from Georgia. The qualifying score is a minimum floor; aim as high as possible to keep more college options open.
What is the total fee for MBBS in Georgia for 6 years?
Annual tuition ranges from $4,800 (BAU) to $6,500 (GAU). Including hostel, food, insurance, and personal expenses, the all-in 6-year total typically falls between ₹40 lakh and ₹54 lakh depending on university and accommodation type. One-time registration fees and flights are additional.
Do Indian students need a visa to study MBBS in Georgia?
Yes. Indian students require a valid student visa to study in Georgia. You will need to apply for a Georgian student visa after receiving your admission offer letter. Your university's international office will guide you through the visa application process, which typically takes 2–4 weeks.
Is IELTS required for MBBS in Georgia?
No IELTS or TOEFL is required for admission to the English-medium MBBS programs at NMC-approved Georgian universities. However, a valid NEET qualifying score is mandatory for Indian students as per National Medical Commission guidelines to become eligible for licensing and medical practice in India after graduation.
Which Georgia university is best for FMGE / NExT for Indian students?
Georgian American University (GAU) is consistently cited as the top FMGE performer, with BAU International University and Georgian National University (SEU) also showing strong recent profiles. Students should compare tuition fees, hostel facilities, coaching support, and overall academic outcomes before deciding.
Is MBBS in Georgia recognised by NMC?
Yes — many medical universities in Georgia are recognised by the National Medical Commission and listed in international medical directories (WDOMS). Indian students with a valid NEET qualification can pursue MBBS in Georgia and become eligible for licensing pathways in India, subject to current NMC regulations. Always verify the latest university approval status on the NMC Official Website before taking admission.
Is Georgia better than Russia for MBBS for Indian students?
Both are popular destinations. Georgia is often preferred for its modern infrastructure, easier admission process, European-style education, English-medium teaching throughout, and comparatively milder climate. Russia offers a wider range of government medical universities and lower tuition in many cases — but clinical rotations there are typically in Russian. The better option depends on budget, learning environment, recognition, clinical exposure, and long-term career plans.
What is the biggest risk when choosing MBBS in Georgia?
The biggest risk is choosing a university without properly verifying its NMC recognition, academic quality, clinical exposure, and FMGE/NExT track record. University quality varies widely within Georgia — a poor choice can erase every country-level FMGE advantage. Always verify FMGE data independently before trusting any agent, and budget holistically including living costs, not just tuition.
